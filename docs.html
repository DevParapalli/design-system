( PROXIMA · DESIGN FOUNDATION )

Dark first, quiet until it matters.

This is the design system behind my homelab console. Dark is the default because that is what I prefer, but the system ships four accents, two themes and two backgrounds, and every combination is a real supported option. Everything below is live. Use the panel in the bottom right to switch, and watch the components and the swatches follow along.

( PRINCIPLES )

Six rules, and one thing that is up to you

The rules below hold whichever look you pick. What you pick is your call. Indigo on ink dark is only the default. Teal on flat light is just as valid, and if that suits the thing you are building, use it. Sixteen combinations, all of them checked.

01

Neutrals come from the ramp

Never #000, never #fff, never untinted grey. On dark the ramp leans blue, on light it stays cool. Both feel like a place rather than an inverted settings panel.

02

One accent, your pick of four

Indigo, teal, ember or lime. Choose one at the root and let it own active states, primary buttons and focus rings. The rule is one accent per app, not one accent for everyone.

03

State twice over

Status is a tint you feel and a word you read. Never colour on its own, never a bare label. This is what keeps the palette swappable without losing meaning.

04

Depth from glass, or not at all

Things lift with blur and translucency instead of shadows, and only the top layer gets a real shadow. Flat mode drops the blooms and the system still works.

05

Hide the detail

A row is one line until you open it. Density is something you ask for, not something the page does to you.

06

Texture, barely

Five percent grain, soft colour blooms underneath, both optional. Atmosphere is fine. Noise is not.

rule: pick a combination, set it once at the root, and stay there. Every rule above survives the switch. Your taste decides which of the sixteen you ship.

( LAYOUT )

Three grids, one question

Every page here is built from three grids. Which one you want comes down to a single question: are the tiles in a row about the same height?

1 · Mosaic, .mosaic and .m-*

Twelve columns. Each tile says how wide it is and the rows line up. Good for a row of numbers or a set of cards that are all about the same size.

2 · Columns, .cols-main and .stack

Two columns that run down the page independently, so a short chart above a long table never leaves a hole next to it. This is what the overview page uses.

3 · Panes, .panes and .pane

Two equal halves. You edit on the left and see the result on the right. The right half takes .pane-sticky so it stays put while the form scrolls. See the alert studio.

( FORMS )

Fields, selects, setting rows

Single line inputs are capsules. Anything multi line relaxes to a softer corner. The label sits above, help and error text sit below. A setting row pairs a label and a sentence of explanation with its control, and it is the unit every preferences screen is made of. All of it is working on the settings page.
